Abstract: Predicting part distortion before fabrication remains challenging in laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), while thermomechanical simulation is computationally expensive for repeated design evaluations. This study presents a two-branch graph attention network (GAT) to predict three-dimensional surface displacement directly from STL-derived graphs. The first branch learns geometric relationships among connected vertices of a densified STL surface mesh, while the second incorporates 12 part-level descriptors of mass distribution, build-plate contact, overhang, size, and shape. Displacement labels from ANSYS Additive Print simulations are mapped onto STL vertices using Gaussian-kernel weighted averaging. Evaluated on 117 parts using five-fold cross-validation split by part, the model achieves an R^2 of 0.946 and a mean absolute error of 0.0178 mm for displacement magnitude. Relative to a node-wise multilayer perceptron, GAT improves R^2 from 0.913 to 0.946; for build-direction displacement, R^2 increases from 0.903 to 0.939. GAT also outperforms GraphSAGE (R^2 = 0.936) and a graph convolutional network (R^2 = 0.934) across all five folds. Ablation experiments show that removing mass-distribution descriptors reduces magnitude R^2 to 0.900, while removing build-plate contact and overhang descriptors reduces it to 0.920. These results demonstrate the value of combining local mesh connectivity with global geometric descriptors to predict LPBF distortion, enabling faster design iteration and earlier identification of distortion-prone geometries.

Abstract: Irregular multivariate time series forecasting supports healthcare monitoring, environmental analysis, and activity recognition. Channel-independent (CI) approaches capture each variable's temporal patterns but do not explicitly use observations from other variables. Channel-dependent (CD) modeling exploits relationships among variables, yet asynchronous measurements make these relationships difficult to learn. Incorporating cross-variable information while preserving each variable's irregular history therefore remains a key challenge. We propose Temporal-Reference Complementary Forecasting (TRCF), a framework that augments a compact CI forecaster with a complementary CD branch. ANCHOR aggregates observed values and timestamps at shared temporal locations, retaining observation support to form compact CI features. BRIDGE constructs complementary features through history completion. It first estimates each variable's historical values from its own observations, then uses differences between other variables' observed and estimated values at nearby times to correct missing entries. The completed history preserves observed values and is encoded to supply context alongside the direct CI representation. Finally, the two representations are fused, combined with the query-time embedding, and passed to a shallow multilayer perceptron for forecasting. Experiments on four real-world datasets show that TRCF achieves the lowest mean squared error and mean absolute error in the main benchmark comparison, while reducing measured peak inference memory by 17.28% to 87.98% relative to APN.

Abstract: Interpolating a spatial field, such as daily maximum temperature, from sparse, irregularly located observations recurs across the environmental and health sciences. Standard Gaussian-process kriging refits hyperparameters and solves an $O(N^3)$ system for every new field. When many related fields are observed, such as daily maps from one sensor network, neural processes (NPs) instead learn the interpolation map from past fields and predict a new field in one forward pass. Joint-predictive variants, which sample whole fields rather than independent per-location values, typically use self-attention over context and query points, costing $O(K^2)$ in the combined point count $K$ and limiting practical use to a few thousand points. State-space models offer a linear-time alternative, but a direct state-space adaptation compresses the context into one hidden state before reading queries, reducing what the decoder sees. We propose MambaFlowNP, a linear-compute joint-predictive NP trained with conditional flow matching. A multi-directional Mamba-2 scan reads context and query tokens in one shared sequence, so each query's velocity is read from its own scan position rather than a compressed context summary, and because queries only read the scan state, predictions are consistent across query sets. MambaFlowNP is competitive with the strongest attention-based joint-predictive baselines on synthetic Matérn Gaussian-process benchmarks, and on regional to continental-scale NOAA temperature interpolation it attains the lowest RMSE and CRPS and near-nominal coverage on every tier, while running 114--784$\times$ faster than the strongest joint-predictive baseline. A single forward pass scales to $10^6$ points on one NVIDIA L40S GPU in under a second. This makes joint prediction feasible for continental-scale, irregularly sampled sensor networks; predictive quality is evaluated up to $K=5{,}580$.

Abstract: Continuous diffusion models generate discrete data through stochastic dynamics in an embedding space. We consider jump diffusions that additionally reset coordinates to embeddings of categorical values. A coordinate's own corrupted state can make prediction easy, potentially reducing the pressure to learn dependencies between coordinates. We study leave-one-out (LOO) prediction, in which each coordinate is predicted from the corrupted states of the other coordinates. We show that, under stated assumptions, jump diffusions with complete coordinate resets and state-independent clocks preserve the prescribed marginals when reset destinations use exact LOO probabilities and the drift uses the full endpoint posterior. Abstract: Attention in large language models (LLMs) scores every query against every key with a full dot product. We investigate whether this dot product must be computed completely for attention to be effective. We propose Hidden Sparse Attention (HiSA), which keeps the query and key projections intact but only computes a partial dot product, following a static, parameter-free rule set by the token positions: attention becomes sparse in the hidden (feature) dimension, an axis much less explored than token sparsity, rather than over tokens as in almost all sparse attention methods. Based on the structure of rotary position embeddings (RoPE), we decompose keys and queries into parts which can be omitted when computing attention. By training LLMs from scratch with HiSA, we find that this partial dot product does not hurt but improves performance compared with the complete product while computing only about a third of the query-key products. During inference HiSA improves decoding speed and shrinks memory footprint. We also extend HiSA to FlashAttention-3 kernels for training and inference. Finally, we motivate our approach by showing that dense attention learns to suppress the attention score parts that HiSA drops. Code is available at https://github.com/ambroise-b/hisa.

Abstract: Three-dimensional point clouds are a fundamental data modality, and a large family of machine learning architectures has been developed to respect their natural symmetries to rigid motions and permutations. A principled way to compare these architectures, which vary widely in complexity and cost, is through their expressive power. That is, an architecture is complete if it can distinguish any two inputs not related by the symmetries it respects. Simple distance-based message-passing networks are known to be incomplete, and while there exist complete alternatives, they typically rely on computationally intensive mechanisms like high-order representations of the rotation group or higher-order Weisfeiler-Leman-type tests.
  In this work, we devise a novel, simple, equivariant message-passing architecture for point clouds, and prove that it is complete. We complement our theoretical result with experiments showing that our architecture separates challenging point clouds that distance-based message passing provably cannot distinguish. Our results suggest that high-order equivariant features may not be necessary to enhance the expressivity of point cloud architectures, and can help settle completeness for other equivariant architectures for which such results are currently unknown. We give initial evidence for this by proving completeness of a slightly modified version of SEGNN.
