Abstract: Amine-impregnated sorbents with the highest nominal CO2 capacity are frequently the least deployable, as amine beyond the pore-volume limit blocks active sites. Yet multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) for direct air capture (DAC) treats formulation search as unconstrained. We encode the pore-filling boundary as an explicit outcome constraint, c(x) = tanh(1 - phi_hat) - tau, and use it as a diagnostic instrument on a hand-curated, DOI-traceable benchmark of 618 solid-amine formulations from 43 publications. Unconstrained MOBO attains higher nominal capacity (3.639 +/- 0.815 vs. 2.964 +/- 0.728 mmol/g, p = 1.0e-11), but across 100 seeds 63.2% of its Pareto-optimal candidates violate the pore-filling boundary versus 39.3% under the constraint: it is rewarded for proposing candidates outside the pore-confined design regime. The constraint steers the front to lower loadings (55.8 vs. 65.2 wt%) and larger pores. The boundary is corroborated once apparatus effects are harmonized hierarchically: 77.8% of kinetic variance is between-laboratory, and pore filling carries a significant kinetic penalty (p = 0.002). Standard hypervolume over-credits feasibility-agnostic methods by 13.9-16.1% (vs. 11.1% constrained), and three surrogate-based analyses---the constrained front, a regeneration-energy third objective, and harmonized kinetics---consistently point to a conservative deployable window at pore filling phi_hat ~ 0.4-0.5 (45-55 wt% loading). The benchmark and associated analysis outputs accompany this submission.

Abstract: Single-cell-informed deconvolution provides cell-type-resolved features for clinical bulk RNA cohorts. Most existing methods predict the proportions $π_k$ of the $K$ constituent cell types (CTs). Fewer predict cell-type-specific expression (CTSE) $μ_{kg}$ of gene $g$ in CT $k$, which can capture cell states that proportions miss, but recent benchmarks find it much harder. We explain this gap with closed-form accuracy bounds. For CTSE, we derive an $R^2$ ceiling for per-gene linear estimators. For proportions, the best linear estimator, which pools evidence across genes, has a closed-form $R^2$, and a computable lower bound on it predicts which CTs are learnable. We study targeted deconvolution of 106 biologically curated (CT, gene) pairs, found post hoc to have higher learnability scores. On overall survival in non-small cell lung cancer, estimation difficulty does not predict prognostic value across these targets. Yet a sparse survival model on deconvolved features, mostly CTSE, predicts survival as well as a sparse model on the bulk genes themselves, while attributing its signal to specific cell types. Some deconvolved CTSE features reveal prognostic signal not seen in the corresponding bulk gene.
